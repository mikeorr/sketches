How to play

Spysol is like the Spider card game but instead of playing cards it has colored squares.

The two suits are shown in the "Models" section at the bottom. Each suit has ranks 1-9 in a different color range. You have four of each suit, or 72 cards total.

The tableau starts with several cards dealt randomly into seven rows. The remaining cards form the stock for later play.

To move, drag a card to another row. All the cards to the right of it will move too. When you assemble a complete suit like one of the models, it will automatically be removed from the tableau and you'll get a point at the top. When you remove all suits (8 points), you win the game.

If you want to undo a move, do the opposite move.

When you run out of desired moves, press "Draw" to deal a random card to each row from the stock. You can do this six times.

The Spider card game has additional rules this version doesn't enforce. In Spider you can move only a descending sequence, and you must move it to a column whose bottom card is the next-higher rank or an empty column. For instance, you can move 4-3-2 to a column ending in 5. Rules differ on whether all these cards must be the same suit. And in Spider you can't have an empty column when pressing "Draw". Spysol doesn't enforce these restrictions.

You can change the suit colors anytime by pressing "Change Colors": the suits will switch to two new random color ranges. To start a new game, press "New Game" or reload the page.

About Spysol

Spysol was created by Mike Orr. This is version 4.

It's written in modern vanilla Javascript and CSS. The only library is random-seedable by Chris Akroyd to shuffle the cards and choose the suit colors. The suit colors are managed by CSS. The cards are HTML <data>'s with classes for the suit and rank. Two CSS variables set the base hue for the two suits. These feed into HSL color values for the suit-rank shades.
